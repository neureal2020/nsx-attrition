Neurosurgery Residency Attrition

About 1 in 14 neurosurgery residents leaves the field before graduating. Of residents who entered in 2011–2018 and have had time to finish, 7.1% did not graduate from any neurosurgery residency (95% CI 6.0–8.4%).

105 residents left their program but continued neurosurgery elsewhere. That is 32% of everyone who left a program, almost the same as Agarwal's 33.6%. We count these as transfers, not attrition; studies that count them report higher rates.

5. Region, program size and individual programs

The confidence intervals overlap, so neither region nor program size makes a clear difference. Agarwal likewise found no regional difference.

6. How our method compares with earlier studies

Earlier studies used match lists, society or board directories, or numbers reported by programs to ACGME or AAMC. We rebuilt every program's roster, year by year, from its own web pages (live and archived) and then checked each resident against an outside source. The table shows what each study used and what it found.

Our data, measured the other studies' way

  • Counting transfers and deaths as attrition, like Agarwal: 11.5% of 2011–18 entrants (Agarwal: 11.0% for 2005–10).
  • Per year, counting transfers, like ACGME-based studies: 1.7% (published: 2.1–2.6%).
  • Excluding transfers, like Haruno: 7.1% (Haruno: 10.4% for 2001–18; Agarwal recomputed: 7.7%).

The main difference from program-reported sources is coverage: a web roster shows only who a program chose to list, so we cross-checked the count against ACGME totals every year and every entering class against the national match. Gender could not be analysed because the rosters do not record it.

Methods and limits

  • Who is counted: residents who entered an ACGME neurosurgery program in 2011–2024. Excluded: later entrants, pre-2011 entrants, fellows listed on rosters, and the military program (no public rosters).
  • Attrition: left neurosurgery without graduating by 30 June 2025. Transfers to another neurosurgery program, deaths and career changes after graduating are not attrition. A resident who transferred and then left is counted once, at the last program.
  • Range: if every "destination not found" resident had quietly transferred, attrition would be 5.3% overall; if twelve matched residents we could not name had all left, 6.9%. The main overall figure is 6.5%.
